# Anirban-s-Bicycle-Taillight

A Bicycle Taillight with a IMU to detect braking and much more, Still refining the Idea

# 2026-09-05: Schematic Designing

**Total time spent: 4 hours**

So this session's task is planning out the circuit and making the schematic. I started by importing the USB-C receptacle, then I grounded the shield and GND pins, connected VCC to a “VBUS-5V” net, added decoupling capacitors between VBUS-5V and GND, and added 5.1K pull-down resistors between CC1/CC2 and GND. Now the TP4057; I haven’t worked with just the IC before so after a bit of googling I placed a 5K resistor between PIN 6 (PROG) and GND, connected the blue LED's anode to VBUS-5V and its cathode to PIN 5 (STDBY#) through a 1K resistor, connected the red LED’s anode to VBUS-5V and its cathode to PIN 1 (CHRG#) through a 2.2K resistor, and obviously PIN 2 (GND) to GND, PIN 4 (VCC) to VBUS-5V, PIN 3 (BAT) to BAT+. For the battery, I am just using a 2-pin header and connecting PIN 1 to BAT+, PIN 2 to GND and also a 10uF decoupling cap between them. The Power Switch is pretty simple too; I am just using an AO3407 MOSFET I chose before and connecting the gate to GND through the 1P DIP switch, pulling the gate up to BAT+ with a 100K resistor to prevent it from floating, and obviously connecting the source to BAT+ and the drain to a new net named VCC. Now that these are out of the way, I am planning to make the accelerometer and MCU part, but the problem is I haven’t worked with any accelerometer or an ATtiny and have no idea how to use them, so I am just gonna do some research, read some datasheets and get back to the circuit. Okay, so the first thing I need before continuing is 3.3V because the accelerometer (LIS3DHTR) strictly accepts only 3.3V. For 3.3V, I am adding an XC6206P332MR CMOS Voltage regulator and giving it input from the VCC net and taking output to the VBUS-3.3V net, while making sure to add some decoupling caps. Now I can move to the accelerometer. So I have connected PIN1/PIN14 (VCC) to VBUS-3.3V and PIN5/PIN12 (GND) to GND. Other than that, I am pulling PIN10 (RES) to GND and PIN8 (CS) and PIN7 (SDO/SA0) to 3.3V. For the I2C communication between the LIS3DHTR and the ATtiny, I have connected the LIS3DH’s SDA to the ATtiny’s PB1 and SCL to PB0, while also adding pull-up resistors between 3.3V and both data lines. The Control switches were really simple; I just connected one side's terminals to GND and the other side’s terminals to nets like SW1, SW2, etc. Now, for the LEDs, I am planning to use 6 LEDs and divide them into groups of 2 so I can control these groups independently for more software control. To drive them, I will be using the AO3400 MOSFETs. So I have placed the LEDs and after some math I have connected their anodes directly to VCC, and their cathodes to the MOSFETs' Drains through 47-ohm resistors. I have connected the MOSFETs' gates to nets like MOS-1, MOS-2, etc. and their sources to ground, while also adding a 10K pull-down resistor between their gates and sources. Now comes to the last part the ATtiny. For power I have connected PIN1 (VDD) to VCC and PIN14 (GND) to GND with a decoupling cap between them. Then are the Control switches' nets; for those I have connected SW-1 to PIN7 (TOSC2/PB2), SW-2 to PIN6 (TOSC2/PB1), SW-3 to PIN5 (PA7), and SW-4 to PIN4 (PA6). For the LEDs I have connected MOSFET gate nets to PIN 11, 12, and 13 through 100-ohm resistors. For programming the MCU I have added a 3-pin header and connected PIN1 to GND, PIN2 to the ATtiny’s UPDI pin and PIN3 to VCC. # 2026-09-03: Parts Selaction

**Total time spent: 1.45 hours**

So my task for this session is to select the main parts I will use in the Taillight. First is the battery. My plan is to browse sites like Robu or MakerBazar until I find a good option. The WLY602040 3.7V 400mAh 1S LiPo Battery sounds like a solid option, so I am picking that.
Now the MCU. Personally I haven’t worked with any small MCUs, but after some research and lots of datasheets I have selected the ATtiny1614 SOIC-14 footprint cause I can easily hand-solder it to the PCB. I am a little confused about how and what LEDs to use. I had some options in mind, like using multiple 1-watt LEDs so they can be bright enough when braking is detected and dim using PWM, or using a few small LEDs and one big LED so the small LEDs can be efficient normally and I can use the big one when braking is detected. After some research on how bright and efficient these LEDs are, I found out that something like 500mW SMD LEDs should be bright and efficient enough for both normal and braking lighting. So I picked some 500mW red SMD LEDs in the 5730 footprint. I am also adding some AO3400 MOSFETs so the MCU can control the LEDs without getting fried.
Now the accelerometer. My first plan was to hand solder everything to a PCB using a soldering iron, but I couldn't find any accelerometer in a hand solderable footprint. So I did some research on ways of soldering tiny SMD footprints and the most viable option was a sand bath because it didn’t require buying any other equipment. After I had this out of the way I searched for a low power accelerometer, and after reading lots of datasheets my final decision was the LIS3DH. But when I went to add it to cart I found out it was out of stock. Fortunately, not only was a LIS3DH module or breakout board in stock, but it was also cheaper than the bare chip. Don’t know how the math works, but the good thing is that I can extract that chip from the breakout using the same sand bath method.
I am also thinking to add some controls so I can toggle some features on or off, but I was a little confused because this is a fairly small device. I can’t add normal toggle switches, and even those DIP switches would look weird. After some digging I found some small half-pitch DIP switches that sit flat without the switch poking out. These are some good options because they sit flat with a protective film so it will be easy to seal with the enclosure and make it water resistant. So I added a 4-P switch for controls and a 1-P switch for on and off, and also added an AO3407 MOSFET because the switches can only handel about 25ma. Also I had to purchase 2 MOSFETs cause Robu doesn't allow adding products below a certain price.
I also think that I should integrate a charging circuit. This would make the CAD way easier because I will not have to open and get the battery out everytime I want to charge it. So I am adding a TP4057 charging IC along with some red and blue LEDs for charge indicator and a USB-C receptacle. Almost forgot about tweezers to place the parts, for which I am adding an ESD-15 curved tweezers.
One more thing that is troubling me is that the sand bath will have no temperature control, and normal solder paste needs 183 degrees Celsius to melt and more to properly flow. Without any temprature control, it has high chances of getting to the point of burning the components. Searching for the solution to this problem, I think I need Tin-bismuth (Sn42/Bi58) solder paste which melts at 138 degrees Celsius.
